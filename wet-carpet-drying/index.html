Wet Carpet Drying Help in Cambridge

Carpet holds far more water than it looks like it does. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Cambridge extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. If the water came out of a clean supply line and we reach it fast, the carpet can normally stay down while it dries. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. Both layers, carpet and pad, are removed when the water was sewage or came in from outside flooding.

Signs to Look For

  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • A carpet corner where the backing comes apart from the face when pulled up
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
Repair or Replace

What Stays and What Goes

It depends on the material, how long it stayed wet and how clean the water was. We test before we decide, and we tell you why.

How We Fix It

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. A wand by itself leaves much more behind, so this step cuts the drying time afterward.

3

Dry Every Layer

Some jobs call for floating the carpet on a cushion of air, others for lifting it and taking the pad out. Our air movers and dehumidifiers keep going until meters show a dry carpet and a dry subfloor.

4

Clean and Reinstall

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.

Water Held in the Pad

Water many times heavier than the pad itself can sit inside it. The carpet over it and the floor under it keep getting damp from the pad, even once the surface seems dry.

Mold Under the Carpet

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Stains From Furniture

When carpet is wet, the finish on wooden legs and the rust from metal glides transfer into it. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It moves under walls and into the next room, and the tack strip starts soaking it up.

24 to 48 hours

Odor and Mold Begin

Bacteria in the damp pad start producing that sour wet carpet smell. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Day 3 to 7

Carpet Starts Failing

As the latex bonding the backing gives out, the carpet delaminates and starts to ripple. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.
