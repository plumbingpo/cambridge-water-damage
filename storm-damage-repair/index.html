Heavy rainstorms, hurricanes, tropical storms and flash floods can do serious water damage to homes and buildings in Cambridge. Gaps in siding, broken glass and roof damage all let wind-blown rain into the building. Once the roof leaks, water saturates wall cavities, ceilings and insulation. During a flash flood, drains cannot keep up and water ends up in the lowest floors. Cambridge Restoration provides emergency storm response: we stop more water from getting in, pump out what is standing and begin restoring the property immediately.

Storm repair with our team starts with emergency tarps and boards to block more water, then moves through extraction and structural drying to the rebuild of damaged parts. One problem tends to trigger the next after a storm, as a roof opening admits water, wet ceilings and walls break down, and poor drying leads to mold growth. We address all of these connected issues under one coordinated restoration plan.

After storm damage your insurer will want records, and we supply them: photos, moisture readings, written damage assessments and a detailed scope of work. Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Make sure the attic is properly ventilated and insulated to prevent ice dams
  • Clean out downspouts and gutters regularly, which lets heavy rainfall drain away as it should
  • Have your roof checked annually and fix vulnerable areas ahead of the stormy months
  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
  • Photograph your property once a year so its condition is on record for insurance purposes
  • Cut back limbs that hang over or close to the house so wind cannot throw them into it

Signs It Is Time for Storm Damage Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Downspouts knocked off and gutter runs hanging after high wind
  • A musty smell indoors in the days after heavy rain
  • From ground level, the roof shows shingles torn off, broken or raised
  • Water in the lowest level after storm drains back up or overflow
  • Water pooling in the attic, around the chimney or near skylights
  • Flashing that is bent, loose or missing at roof edges, valleys and vents
  • Water stains that show up on ceilings or walls during or after a storm
